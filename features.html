What it measures

Ten parameters on a schedule, with no test kits and no matching colours by eye.

  • Alkalinity
  • Calcium
  • Magnesium
  • Nitrate
  • Phosphate

Measured by colour in the optical bench. Reagent formulation is in development; six reagent bottles ship with the Beta Kit.

  • pH
  • ORP
  • Salinity
  • Temperature
  • Dissolved oxygen

Read continuously. pH, ORP, salinity and temperature probes are included; the dissolved oxygen probe is sold separately at launch.

The parts

Four modules do the work. Pick one.

Optical bench

Three LEDs at 540, 615 and 880 nm shine through a clear cuvette onto a light sensor. How much light the sample absorbs becomes the reading.

Pump

A peristaltic pump meters the tank sample and every reagent through one shared path, in both directions. Liquid only ever touches the tubing.

Rotary valve

A stepper-driven rotor selects one of eight ports: six reagents, the sample and waste. It homes itself before every run.

Mainboard

One custom board runs everything on an ESP32-S3 with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth: isolated probe inputs, two stepper drivers, a CAN bus for expansion and USB-C for recovery.

Lighting

Thirty-three LEDs under a diffuser at the base and a separately lit logo. Set any colour, or let them show what Wavie is doing.

Tap a colour or swipe the picture. On the real device, colours are set in the app.

Size and connections

184 mm tall and 88 mm square. Everything plugs in at the back.

Small enough for a sump cabinet or a shelf beside the tank. Banana for scale.

24 V power, the CAN expansion bus, and inputs for temperature, pH, ORP, salinity and dissolved oxygen.

Open hardware

Wavie is built to be opened, understood and repaired by the person who owns it.

The design files and documentation are published on GitHub under a non-commercial licence: build, repair and modify your own, but not for resale. The wiki explains how each part works in far more detail than this page.

We will sell replacement parts at cost. Reagents are formulated and sold only by ReefMetrics, which is what keeps the project running.
