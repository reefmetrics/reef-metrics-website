Colorimetric Chemistry

Reagent-Based Tests

Six reagents feed an internal optical cell. Wavie meters sample and reagent, mixes, and reads the result across three wavelengths — calcium, alkalinity, and magnesium by automated titration, nitrate and phosphate by colour development. No test kits, no color-matching by eye.

Under the Hood

The Parts of Wavie

Five subsystems, each purpose-built for the reef. Open any part to see how it works and what it looks like up close.

Optics

The Optical Bench

Wavie’s spectrophotometer is a multi-wavelength optical bench. Three narrow-band LEDs — 540 nm green, 615 nm orange, and 880 nm near-infrared — fire one at a time through a fixed-geometry cuvette while a high-dynamic-range light sensor reads exactly how much light makes it through. How much of each wavelength gets absorbed is what turns a colour change into a number: the same colorimetric method a lab uses, shrunk to fit a cabinet.

Fluid Path

The Pump & Valve

A single bidirectional peristaltic pump meters every fluid in the system to hundredths of a millilitre — the tank sample, all six reagents, and waste. It works through a purpose-built rotary valve that selects one of eight positions: six reagent lines, the sample line, and waste. One shared path, re-homed before every run so the valve always knows exactly where it is.

Electronics

The Mainboard

Every subsystem answers to one custom board. An ESP32-S3 brings Wi-Fi and Bluetooth with 16 MB of storage and 8 MB of RAM. Around it sit three galvanically isolated probe front-ends, dual stepper drivers for the pump and valve, a CAN transceiver ready for future expansion modules, and USB-C for worst-case firmware recovery.

Lighting

The RGB Ring

A dedicated board carries a ring of addressable RGB LEDs, with a second independent channel backlighting the ReefMetrics logo. Together they tell you what Wavie is doing — priming, testing, or reporting — from across the room. Both zones are yours to set: pick any colour and brightness to match your tank, or let them react to the run in progress.

Enclosure

The Chassis

Optics and pump up top, fluidic selection in the middle, electronics at the base — stacked into a single sealed tower barely taller than a banana. It tucks into a sump cabinet, sits on a shelf, or mounts to a wall. Everything connects at the back: 24V power, the CAN expansion bus, and a labeled, keyed input for every probe.
